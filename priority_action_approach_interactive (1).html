Priority Action Approach

Interactive learning flow • Basic First Aid (formerly OFA Level 1)

Learning goal: Use the Priority Action Approach to move from scene assessment to patient assessment, critical action, transport/referral decisions, and ongoing assessment. This activity is an instructional aid and should be used with the current course materials and instructor direction.

1. Scene Assessment

Before touching the worker: Is the scene safe?

Think: Hazards → Mechanism of injury → Number of patients

Ask yourself:
  • Are there hazards that could make you another patient?
  • What happened? Consider the mechanism of injury.
  • How many patients are involved?
  • Do workplace emergency procedures need to be activated?
STOP. Do not enter or continue assessment if doing so creates an unreasonable risk to you or the patient. Follow workplace emergency procedures and obtain the appropriate help.
Scene safe? Continue to the primary survey.
↓

2. Primary Survey + Critical Interventions

A rapid assessment to identify and address immediate life-threatening conditions.

First question: Is the worker responsive?

Unresponsive worker

Immediately assess breathing and circulation according to your current Basic First Aid training.

Critical intervention: Activate the emergency response system and begin CPR/AED according to your training. Continue until appropriate transfer of care or other stopping criteria apply.
Continue the primary survey, identify immediate threats, provide critical interventions within your scope, and reassess frequently.

Responsive worker

Continue the primary survey. Identify immediate threats and provide critical interventions within your training and scope.

Act now. Treat the immediate life threat first, then reassess before moving on.
The primary survey is complete enough to make the next decision: what level of medical care or transport is needed?
↓

3. Transport / Medical-Aid Decision

Does the worker require urgent transport, medical assessment, or treatment and return to work?

Rapid transport: Activate/confirm the appropriate emergency response and prepare the worker for transport according to your training and workplace procedures. Continue reassessment and critical care while awaiting transfer.
Medical aid: Arrange the appropriate medical referral/transport and continue to monitor the worker while awaiting care.
Treatment / return to work: Provide care within scope, reassess, provide appropriate follow-up instructions, and complete required workplace documentation.
↓

4. Secondary Survey

Once immediate threats have been addressed, gather more information and reassess the worker.

  • Obtain an appropriate history, including the chief complaint and relevant circumstances.
  • Check and record appropriate vital signs as taught in your course.
  • Perform an appropriate physical assessment for the worker's condition.
  • Document findings and care as required.
  • Reassess regularly — a worker's condition can change.
↓

Ongoing Assessment

Priority Action Approach is not “one and done.” Continue to reassess, recognize deterioration, repeat critical actions when necessary, and update the transport/medical-aid plan.
